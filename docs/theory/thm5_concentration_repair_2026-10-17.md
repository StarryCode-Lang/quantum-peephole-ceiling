## 1. Status inherited from the audit

`docs/theory/formal_results.md` §"Candidate Theorem 5" states, for
$X = |\mathcal{A}_{\text{adj}}(C)|$,

$$
\Pr\!\left[X \ge \mathbb{E}[X] + t\right] \le \exp\!\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2N}\right),
\qquad N = n(d-1),
$$

and records the proof as **incomplete** because the displayed constant assumes
$N$ independent per-wire choices, whereas the generator draws a random
two-qubit matching in each layer, coupling the endpoint choices. The audit's
conclusion — *E30 agreement with the expectation does not validate this tail
bound* — is correct and is retained here.

---

## 2. Why the bounded-differences proof cannot be repaired as written

McDiarmid's inequality needs a product space of independent variables
$\xi_1,\dots,\xi_N$ and constants $c_i$ with
$|f(\xi) - f(\xi^{(i)})| \le c_i$. The obstruction is not the labels; it is the
matching.

1. **Matching as one variable per layer.** Let $M_L$ be layer $L$'s matching.
   Changing $M_L$ can change the gate carried by every matched wire, hence up to
   $2n$ adjacent-pair indicators. So $c_{M_L} = \Theta(n)$, and with $d$ such
   variables $\sum_i c_i^{2} = \Theta(dn^{2})$ — an exponent $\Theta(dn^2)$
   instead of $N = \Theta(nd)$. The resulting bound is vacuous in exactly the
   regime of interest ($\mathbb{E}[X] \approx nd\,p_{\text{cancel}} \ll n\sqrt{d}$).
2. **Matching as independent per-edge coins.** Encoding $M_L$ by independent
   Bernoulli draws on a fixed edge order plus a deterministic conflict rule
   does not help: a single draw can cascade through the greedy matching,
   changing $\Omega(n)$ edges, so the bounded-difference constant is again
   $\Theta(n)$.
3. **Consequence.** There is no product-space bounded-differences certificate
   with constant-size $c_i$ for the random-matching generator. The constant
   $N = n(d-1)$ is therefore not merely unproven — it is not the right scale for
   this generator (see §4).

## 3. Two restricted statements that *are* valid

### Theorem 5-A (fixed matching, independent labels)

Let the matching sequence $(M_1,\dots,M_d)$ be deterministic, and let each of
the $N$ gate slots carry an independent gate label. Then

$$
\Pr\!\left[X \ge \mathbb{E}[X] + t\right] \le \exp\!\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2N}\right).
$$

*Proof.* $X$ is a function of the $N$ independent labels. Changing one label
changes the gate at one slot and, when the slot is matched, the gate on the
edge containing it; in either case at most two wire-consecutive pair indicators
change, so $c_i \le 2$ for all $i$ and $\sum_i c_i^{2} = 4N$. McDiarmid gives
$\exp(-2t^{2}/(4N)) = \exp(-t^{2}/(2N))$. $\blacksquare$

This is precisely the statement the original proof *intended*; the repair is to
say so explicitly by restricting the ensemble.

### Theorem 5-B (conditional tail, random matching)

Let the matching sequence be random and independent of the labels. For every
realization $M$ of the matching sequence,

$$
\Pr\!\left[X \ge \mathbb{E}[X \mid M] + t \;\middle|\; M\right]
\le \exp\!\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2N}\right).
$$

*Proof.* Condition on $M$; the labels remain independent and the argument of
Theorem 5-A applies verbatim. $\blacksquare$

**What 5-B does not give.** The unconditional statement
$\Pr[X \ge \mathbb{E}[X] + t] \le \exp(-t^{2}/(2N))$ requires a second
concentration for $M \mapsto \mathbb{E}[X \mid M]$, which §2 shows cannot be
obtained by bounded differences. Theorem 5-B is therefore a *conditional* tail:
it controls label noise around the conditional mean, not matching noise.

## 4. New evidence: a necessary-condition test on the frozen E30 ensemble

A sub-Gaussian tail with variance proxy $N$ forces
$\operatorname{Var}(X) \le 2N$, because
$\mathbb{E}[(X-\mathbb{E}X)^{2}] = 2\int_0^\infty t\,\Pr[X \ge \mathbb{E}X + t]\,dt
\le 2\int_0^\infty t\,e^{-t^{2}/(2N)}\,dt = 2N$.
This is testable directly on the frozen E30 data, which records `a_adj_raw`
$= X$ per trial.

| Quantity (27 cells, 13,500 trials, 500 trials/cell) | Result |
|---|---|
| Cells violating $\operatorname{Var}(X) \le 2N$ | **0 of 27** |
| $\operatorname{Var}(X)/N$ — min / median / max | 0.0224 / 0.0467 / 0.0667 |
| Dispersion index $\operatorname{Var}(X)/\mathbb{E}[X]$ — min / median / max | 0.9133 / **0.9759** / 1.2254 |
| Implied scale $N_{\text{eff}}/N$ from the empirical extreme tail — min / median / max | 0.0235 / 0.0525 / 0.1118 |

Two readings, both important:

1. **The stated exponent is not excluded.** $\operatorname{Var}(X)$ is
   15–45× *smaller* than $N$, so the bound $\exp(-t^{2}/(2N))$ is not
   contradicted. Thm 5 remains open rather than refuted.
2. **The stated exponent is badly scaled.** The dispersion index is
   $0.98 \pm 0.15$ — X behaves like a **Poisson** count, not like a
   Poisson-binomial over $N$ trials. The empirically implied scale is
   $N_{\text{eff}} \approx \mathbb{E}[X] = n(d-1)\,p_{\text{cancel}}$, i.e.
   smaller than $N$ by exactly the factor $p_{\text{cancel}} \approx 0.02$–$0.11$.
   The published bound is therefore *loose by a factor $1/p_{\text{cancel}}$ in
   the exponent*, which in the large-$n$ regime is the difference between a
   useful and a vacuous statement.

## 5. Proposed replacement: Theorem 5-C (conjecture, data-backed)

> **Theorem 5-C (conjecture).** For the stated LBL generator, $X$ converges in
> distribution to $\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)$ with
> $\lambda = n(d-1)\,p_{\text{cancel}}(n,\rho)$. Consequently, for all $t>0$,
> $$\Pr[X \ge \lambda + t] \;\le\; \exp\!\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2(\lambda + t/3)}\right)$$
> (Bernstein form), which is strictly stronger than the stated
> $\exp(-t^{2}/(2N))$ whenever $p_{\text{cancel}} < 1$.

**Evidence for.** Dispersion index $0.98$ across 27 cells spanning
$n \in \{4,5,8\}$, $d \in \{10,20,40\}$, $\rho \in \{0,0.3,0.6\}$; the implied
tail scale tracks $\mathbb{E}[X]$ rather than $N$ in every cell.

**Falsifier.** Any cell whose dispersion index exceeds $\approx 1.5$, or whose
empirical tail is heavier than the Bernstein form, refutes 5-C. The present test
has 500 trials/cell, so the resolution of the far tail is limited; a
$10^4$-trial replication at three cells would settle it.

**Proof route.** $X = \sum_w X_w$ over wires with $X_w$ counting
wire-consecutive inverse pairs on wire $w$. Under the layer-wise generator the
$X_w$ are only weakly dependent (dependence decays geometrically in the layer
distance because a matching edge couples exactly two wires for exactly one
layer). A Chen–Stein / Poisson-approximation argument with a dependency graph of
bounded degree is the natural route, and it also explains the observed
$p_{\text{cancel}}$-scaling that bounded differences cannot reach.

## 6. Consequent restatement of Corollary 5.1

`formal_results.md` Corollary 5.1 uses the $\exp(-t^{2}/(2N))$ form to obtain a
high-probability Phase-1 reduction bound. That derivation is **not invalidated**
— it is conservative. If 5-C holds, Corollary 5.1 sharpens from

$$
R_1(C) \le O\!\left(\tfrac{1}{g_1^{2}} + \tfrac{1}{g_2 n} + \sqrt{\tfrac{\ln(n)}{nd}}\right)
\quad\text{to}\quad
R_1(C) \le O\!\left(\tfrac{1}{g_1^{2}} + \tfrac{1}{g_2 n} + \tfrac{p_{\text{cancel}}\ln(n)}{d}\right),
$$

i.e. the fluctuation term loses a $\sqrt{n}$ in the denominator. That is the
version worth proving, and it is also the version a certified-barrier theorem
needs as its upper companion.
